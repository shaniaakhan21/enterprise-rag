CONFIDENTIAL — GRANT PROPOSAL EXCERPT (PEER REVIEW COPY)
Proposal ID: NSF-2025-88231
Title: "Adaptive Mesh Refinement for Real-Time Wildfire Spread Simulation"
Principal Investigator: Dr. Helena Wu, Department of Atmospheric Sciences
Requested Amount: $742,000 over 3 years
Institution: not disclosed pending award notification

Specific Aims: This project proposes to develop a GPU-accelerated adaptive
mesh refinement solver capable of simulating wildfire spread at 10-meter
resolution across a 500 km² domain in under 15 minutes, a tenfold speedup
over existing operational models. Aim 1 will develop the core solver;
Aim 2 will validate against the 2023 Canyon Creek Fire dataset; Aim 3 will
build an operational prototype for county-level emergency management use.

Preliminary Data: A prototype tested on the 2021 Dixie Fire perimeter data
achieved 84% spatial overlap with observed fire spread at 6x real-time
speed, using a single A100 GPU.

Reviewer Note (confidential): Strong technical approach but budget for
Aim 3 field deployment seems underscoped relative to described scope;
recommend minor revision before funding decision.
